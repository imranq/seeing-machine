Field note 01How focusing really works

The cornea supplies most of the eye’s fixed focusing power. For near work, the ciliary muscle relaxes its pull on the lens capsule; the lens rounds up and adds power. This is accommodation.

Field note 02A muscle is a vector, not a label

Each extraocular muscle has a primary action, but its pull changes with gaze. The superior and inferior recti also adduct; the obliques help rotate the eye. Cranial nerves III, IV and VI distribute the motor commands.

Field note 03The crossing explains the field loss

At the optic chiasm, fibres from the nasal half of each retina cross. Each optic tract therefore carries the opposite visual hemifield from both eyes. In the cortex, V1 maps that input along the calcarine sulcus, with central vision taking up more cortical area.
